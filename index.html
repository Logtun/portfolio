02 · Featured case study

Axis Robotics Nigeria — Regional Lead (Mar–Oct 2026)

I took over leadership of the Axis Nigeria community on March 31, 2026 (the role began as Axis Ambassador before it became Regional Lead), following my earlier contributions to the Axis ecosystem from January 2026. From there the role grew into regional community development and contributor coordination.

The focus was always more than headcount: building a contributor ecosystem, moving people from joining to actively contributing, and creating real value for Axis. My time in the role ended in October 2026, and I’m proud of what the Axis Nigeria community built together. This is the story of the work behind the growth.

The initiatives

Community growth

From 200+ to 2,000+ members

The Axis Nigeria Discord grew roughly 10×. More importantly, participation grew with it: active members went from 80+ to 700+, so the focus stayed on turning people who joined into active participants and contributors.

Content engine

A monthly content campaign engine

Instead of relying on occasional individual posts, I introduced a structured, repeatable system where community members could learn, create, contribute and get recognized, which kept a steady flow of Axis content coming from the Nigerian community.

X community

Axis NG on X: 0 → 600+ members

I built and managed the Axis NG community page on X from zero to 600+ members, making it the largest X community page among Axis regional communities. It became another dedicated channel for bringing contributors together.

View the community on X ↗

Weekly X Spaces

31+ X Spaces hosted

A weekly X Space covering Axis updates, the Axis Hub, data contribution, community activities, ecosystem news and members’ questions. It became a consistent weekly touchpoint, including a session attended by a member of the Axis team (PL).

Contributor onboarding

Twice-weekly onboarding, 40–100 per session

Dedicated onboarding sessions every Tuesday and Thursday walked members through the Axis Hub and showed them how to complete tasks and contribute trajectories. Sessions typically drew 40–100 participants each: community activity turning directly into contributor onboarding.

Trajectory contribution

Tracked contributors: 70+ → 700+

Because active data contribution is core to Axis’s mission, I shifted the focus to trajectory contribution. Through education, onboarding and contributor-focused activities, tracked trajectory contributors on Discord grew roughly 10×, and the community produced one of the top 10 Axis trajectory contributors.

Participation system

A points system for 300+ members

I created and managed, daily, a community points system that tracked participation and contributions across activities. Bots weren’t allowed on the Discord, so records were kept by hand. Members earned community roles at set thresholds, which gave the community a clear framework for tracking, recognition and reward.

View the points sheet ↗

Community events

Event participation: 30+ → 300+

Weekly activities became core to the regional strategy: educational sessions, gaming events, discussions, content campaigns, contributor activities, recognition, Axis Hub onboarding and hangouts. The goal was recurring chances for members to learn, interact, contribute and stay connected.

Contributor recognition

The Weekly Community Spotlight

A weekly initiative to publicly recognize top contributors based on their participation and contributions, plus recognition for the top trajectory contributors.

Direct onboarding

50+ users onboarded live

In a live onboarding session I introduced 50+ users from another Nigerian Web3 community to Axis, walking them through the actual process of getting started rather than just promoting the project.

Nigerian user base

3,000 → 15,000+ users

During this period the Nigerian Axis user base grew from about 3,000 to 15,000+. That growth isn’t down to any one person; I contributed in part through regional community building, content distribution, education, onboarding, campaigns and contributor activation.
